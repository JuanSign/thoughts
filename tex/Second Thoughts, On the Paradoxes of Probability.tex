\begin{center}
    \vspace*{2em}
    {\Huge \textbf{Second Thoughts}}\\[0.5em]
    {\Large \textit{On the Paradoxes of Probability}}\\[2em]

    {\large \textbf{Juan S. A. Siagian}}\\[0.5em]
    {\normalsize Jakarta, Indonesia \hspace{0.5em} $\cdot$ \hspace{0.5em} \today}\\[3em]
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Gambler's Dice.} Which is more likely: getting at least one six in four rolls of a single die, or getting at least one double six in twenty-four rolls of a pair of dice? What is the probability of each?

    \item \textbf{A Crowded Room.} How many people need to be in a room before there is a better-than-even chance that at least two of them share a birthday? And how many other people need to be in the room before there is a better-than-even chance that someone shares \textit{your} birthday? Ignore leap years and assume every day of the year is equally likely.

    \item \textbf{Three Boxes.} Three boxes each hold two coins. One has two gold coins, one has two silver coins, and one has one of each. You pick a box at random and take out a coin without looking. It is gold. What is the probability that the other coin in the box is also gold?

    \item \textbf{A Positive Test.} A disease affects 1 in 1,000 people. A test for it correctly flags 99\% of people who have the disease, and wrongly flags 1\% of people who do not. You have no symptoms, take the test, and it comes back positive. What is the probability that you have the disease?

    \item \textbf{Three Doors.} On a game show there are three doors. Behind one is a car; behind the other two are goats. You pick a door. The host, who knows where the car is, then opens one of the other two doors to reveal a goat (if both hide goats, he picks one at random) and offers you the chance to switch to the last closed door. If you switch, what is the probability that you win the car?

    \item \textbf{Two Children.} A family is chosen at random from all families with two children, at least one of whom is a boy. What is the probability that both children are boys? Now suppose the family is chosen instead from those where at least one child is a boy born on a Tuesday. What is the probability now? Assume each child is equally likely to be a boy or a girl, and equally likely to be born on any day of the week.

    \item \textbf{Pick Your Pattern.} I choose the pattern heads, heads, heads (HHH), and you choose tails, heads, heads (THH). We flip a fair coin until one of the two patterns appears as three flips in a row, and whoever's pattern appears first wins. What is the probability that you win?

    \item \textbf{Coffee at Noon.} Two friends agree to meet at a café between 12:00 and 13:00. Each arrives at a random time within that hour, independently of the other, and waits 15 minutes before giving up and leaving. What is the probability that they meet?

    \item \textbf{A Broken Stick.} Pick two points at random along a stick and break it at both. What is the probability that the three pieces can form a triangle?

    \item \textbf{Two Patterns.} Flip a fair coin until you get two heads in a row, and count the flips. Then start over and flip until you get a head followed by a tail. On average, how many flips does each one take?

    \item \textbf{Secret Santa.} A group of friends play Secret Santa. Everyone writes their name on a slip of paper, the slips are shuffled in a hat, and each person draws one. What is the probability that nobody draws their own name? Does the answer change much between 10 people and 1,000?

    \item \textbf{The Bus Stop.} Buses arrive at a stop completely at random, independently of each other, ten minutes apart on average. You arrive at the stop at a random moment. On average, how long do you wait for the next bus?

    \item \textbf{Dropping Needles.} A wooden floor is made of parallel planks, each exactly as wide as a needle is long. You drop the needle onto the floor at random. What is the probability that it lands across a gap between two planks?

    \item \textbf{A Hundred Prisoners.} A hundred prisoners are numbered 1 to 100. In a room are 100 boxes, also numbered 1 to 100, and inside each box is one of the prisoners' numbers, shuffled at random. One at a time, each prisoner enters the room, opens up to 50 boxes looking for their own number, and leaves the room exactly as they found it, with no way to signal to the others. If every prisoner finds their number, they all go free; if even one fails, none of them do. They may agree on a strategy beforehand. What is their best chance of going free?

    \item \textbf{Even Rolls.} Roll a fair die until a six comes up. Now consider only the games in which every roll was even. In those games, what is the average number of rolls?

    \item \textbf{A Random Chord.} Draw an equilateral triangle inside a circle, with its three corners on the circle. Now pick a chord of the circle, a straight line joining two points on it, at random. What is the probability that the chord is longer than a side of the triangle?
\end{enumerate}
